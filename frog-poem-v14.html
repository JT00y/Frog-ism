Frog-ism Poem

A WORKSHOP PERFORMANCE

Frog-ism Poem is a workshop performance that seeks to draw public attention to the experiences of women in tech, while highlighting academic research in the field. The first draft, which will also serve as the final presentation for ATCM 6377, will take place on November 18 before 4:00 PM in the Chamber on the third floor of the Edith O’ Donnell Arts and Technology Building.

The workshop performance consists of two parts. In the first part, I will guide participants in folding paper frogs, accompanied by a collective reading of a provided text. In the second part, I will reconstruct the text into a poem using an interactive, web-based poem-generation system driven by body tracking. Through freestyle dance, I will use my arm movements to select fragments of text displayed on the screen, which the system will transform into a reconstructed poem. My collaborator, Rabeeha Adnan, a PhD student in VPAS, will perform the resulting poem for the audience.

Through this performance, I shift the power from the content of the text to bodily gestures and poetry, dissolving the pressure and anxiety experienced by women in tech.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS
  1. Load a text. A PDF, .docx or .txt file, or try the sample text.
  2. Allow the camera and step back until both arms are in frame.
  3. Sweep your arms through the words to pick them. The poem writes itself in the order of your gestures.
  4. Perform. Open ◐ Visual and 🎚 Audio at any time to change type, colors, layers, sounds and tempo live. Enable the drums and the words fall on their beat. The poem has its own window (▢ Poem view), and 🐸 Frog view maps it onto the origami frog. Drag either window to a second screen or projector.
Your text and camera stay on your computer; nothing is uploaded. Use Chrome or Edge, and allow camera access and pop-ups when asked, so the webcam and the poem view can open.

AUDIO TOOLS

A · DRUM MACHINE

B · AMBIENCE + SYNTHS

Every layer is synthesized live. The slider after ON is the layer volume, the last one shapes its tone (filter, tremolo speed or register).

C · WORD-PICK SOUND

Each picked word plays one note. Its pitch comes from the word's letters, mapped onto the chosen scale, plus the Pitch bar. The note goes dry to the mix and through a tempo-synced delay and a reverb.

D · VOICE

Browsers cannot send their built-in voice through effects, so the voice echoes are repeats timed to the tempo.
